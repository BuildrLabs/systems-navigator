# Playbook: Eviction warning or court papers (New York City)
verified_on: 2026-10-16 (partial; see VERIFY section)

## Situation
User received something from a landlord, marshal or court about unpaid rent, ending a tenancy, or leaving the apartment.

## First: identify what the paper is
Ask for a photo and classify. Do not assume.
| Paper | Rough meaning | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Rent demand / pay-or-quit notice | Landlord says rent is owed and asks for payment before filing | High - act this week |
| Notice of termination / notice to quit | Landlord says tenancy is ending | High |
| Notice of Petition + Petition (court papers) | A Housing Court case has been started | Very high - the papers state a deadline to respond; never miss it |
| Court decision / judgment | Court has ruled | Very high - get a lawyer now |
| Warrant of eviction / marshal's notice | Marshal can carry out the eviction on or after a stated date | Emergency - same day |
| No paper, lockout, utilities cut by landlord, belongings removed | May be an illegal eviction | Emergency - same day |

## Key facts the assistant may state (sourced)
- In New York City, free legal help in eviction cases is available under the Right to Counsel law ("Universal Access to Legal Services"). Eligibility depends on household income and case type: nonpayment and holdover cases qualify, as do NYCHA termination-of-tenancy cases. It is available regardless of ZIP code or immigration status. [dir:nyc-right-to-counsel-info]
- Income limits shown on the court system's page as of 2026-10-16: 1 person $29,160; 2 people $39,440; 3 people $49,720 (larger households: send user to the page; limits change). [dir:nyc-right-to-counsel-info]
- Ways to reach a free lawyer: call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline; call the Office of Civil Justice numbers; email civiljustice@hra.nyc.gov with name, phone number and Housing Court case index number; or ask the court clerk/court attorney if already at the courthouse. [dir:nyc-311-tenant-helpline] [dir:nyc-ocj-hotline]
- The NYC Tenant Resource Portal walks tenants through situations including illegal lockouts, in English and Spanish. [dir:nyc-tenant-resource-portal]
- A notice is the start of a process, not by itself an order to leave by the date on the paper. A landlord generally must go through court; locking a tenant out without going through court is treated as an unlawful eviction. The assistant should say this plainly but add that a lawyer should confirm details for the user's specific papers.

## Steps (one sitting each)
1. Take clear photos of every page and the envelope (dates matter). Save them in the vault.
2. Write down every date and number on the papers: deadline to respond, court date, index number, amounts.
3. Do not ignore court papers. If the paper says a response is due, put that date in the calendar with 7/3/1-day reminders.
4. Call for free legal help today (see "Who to call"). If the line is busy or goes to voicemail, leave the voicemail script and try again each morning.
5. Do not move out because of a notice alone, and do not sign anything from the landlord or their lawyer without legal advice.
6. Gather proof: lease, rent receipts or bank records, texts/emails with the landlord, photos of repair problems, any proof of hardship (job loss letter, medical bills).
7. If you can pay part or all of the amount, ask the lawyer or helpline before paying so payments are made in a way that is recorded and does not hurt your case.
8. Ask the lawyer or helpline about rental assistance and other benefits; use ACCESS NYC to check eligibility. [dir:accessnyc]

## Emergency branch (warrant, marshal's notice, lockout)
- Tell the user to call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline immediately and to call the Office of Civil Justice line. If they are locked out or being forced out right now and feel unsafe, call 911.
- Ask them to photograph the notice, the door, and any belongings, and to keep the marshal's or officer's name/badge if given.
- Do not predict whether the eviction can be stopped. Say lawyers can sometimes ask the court for emergency relief and that speed matters.

## Voicemail / call script
"Hello, my name is [name]. I live at [address], [borough]. I received [type of paper] dated [date]. My Housing Court index number is [number, if any]. My court date or deadline is [date]. I need to find out if I qualify for a free lawyer. My phone number is [number]; the best times to reach me are [times]. I can speak [language]."

## What to bring to a lawyer or court
All papers and envelopes, lease, ID, proof of income, rent history, photos/emails/texts, list of everyone in the household.

## Do / Don't
Do: act the same day on court papers; keep copies; ask for an interpreter if needed.
Don't: ignore papers; sign without advice; hand over cash without a receipt; throw anything away.

## The app must not say
- "You will win" / "they can't evict you" / "you don't have to pay"
- Any phone number or address not returned by the directory tool
- That a paper is valid or invalid; only that a lawyer can check
